Owners often fix the staircase and forget the couch. The daily jump count from sofa and bed is usually higher than the stair count, which is why this checklist asks about furniture access in its own blocks. Gates still matter for unsupervised stair racing — especially when the doorbell rings.

How the audit works

You answer eight yes/no questions about the rooms your dog actually uses. The script scores gaps into high, next and later priorities. Printing is optional and stays on your device — nothing is uploaded.

    Why these rooms matter

    Stairs are occasional; sofas and beds are many jumps a day. That is why the stairs guide on this site treats furniture access as the higher daily load, and why AKC discusses dog stairs specifically for small dogs that take a running leap onto the sofa.

    Ramp or dog stairs?

    If the same gear must reach a car or travel between rooms, a folding ramp usually wins. If one sofa is the only destination and floor space is tight, a fixed set of dog stairs can stay put. Nervous dogs often accept a low-angle ramp sooner than open steps — details live in the stairs home-setup article and the ramp length calculator.

    Training without force

    Our ramp training guide uses five stages: sniff, lure each step, side barrier, raise the angle, never push. Pair that with the audit so you do not buy a third ramp before the first one is taught.

    A simple weekend plan

    Saturday morning: run the audit and print the list. Saturday afternoon: install or adjust one high-priority item (usually a gate or a ramp angle). Sunday: one short training session from the ramp training guide — sniff, lure, barrier — without raising the angle yet.

    If the audit flags both stairs and sofa jumps, fix the sofa first when the dog spends evenings on the couch; keep stair gates closed until you can supervise carries. That order follows the same daily-load logic as the stairs home-setup article.

    Gear checklist without new medical claims

    When you buy gear after the audit, stick to the AKC shopping points already summarised on this site: slip-resistant surface, enough width, correct height, weight limit with margin, and a design that matches car versus sofa use. Size the length with the ramp length calculator rather than guessing from photos.

    Carrying on remaining stairs

    When gates are closed and you still need to move between floors, carry with support under the chest and the rear so the long back stays level — the same handling note as in the stairs guide and the step-in harness article. The audit lists this as a later priority so it does not distract from gates and ramps.

    What this tool does not do

    It does not estimate disease risk, recommend supplements, or interpret pain. Sudden reluctance to jump, cry-outs, or rear weakness are veterinary questions — stop the audit and call your clinic.

    FAQ

    What is the Dachshund back-safety home audit?
    A checklist for stairs, ramps, sofa and bed access, and floor grip. It turns your answers into a prioritised fix list you can print. It is a home-setup tool, not a veterinary diagnosis.
    Does this replace a vet visit?
    No. The audit only organises furniture and stair habits already discussed in breed home-setup guides from VCA and AKC. Pain, weakness or sudden mobility changes need a veterinarian.
    Where do the recommendations come from?
    From the same sources linked on our stairs and ramp guides: VCA's Dachshund profile on limiting stair runs and furniture jumps, and AKC notes on ramps versus dog stairs for sofas and cars.
    Can I print the results?
    Yes. After you answer the room questions, use Print priority list. The print view hides the site chrome and keeps the ranked fixes.
