Can Dachshunds Use Stairs? Ramps, Gates and a Dachshund-Friendly Home

Most Dachshund homes have stairs, a sofa and a bed. This guide is about the setup: where a gate helps, how to fix slippery floors, when a ramp beats dog stairs, and how to teach your Dachshund to use one.

Dachshund World Editorial Team  ·  Published May 17, 2026  ·  10 min read

Can Dachshunds use stairs? Many Dachshunds can climb stairs, but breed guides advise keeping it to a minimum. The VCA Dachshund breed profile is direct: Dachshunds should not be allowed to run up and down stairs or jump off furniture. That turns the question into a practical one: how do you set up a home with stairs, a sofa and a bed so your Dachshund rarely needs to climb or jump?

Stairs in your home: gates, traction and carrying

You probably cannot remove the stairs, but you can decide when your Dachshund uses them. Three habits make the biggest difference:

  • A gate at the bottom and top. It stops the dog from racing up and down on its own, especially when the doorbell rings or someone comes home.
  • Grip on every surface. Stair runners, rugs on smooth floors at the bottom of the stairs, and trimmed nails all reduce slipping.
  • Carry with both hands. When you carry a Dachshund up or down, support the chest and the rear together so the long back stays level. Our step-in harness guide covers harnesses that make lifting and handling easier.

Puppies need the most structure, because they are fast and impulsive. Our first-time Dachshund owner guide covers the rest of that first-week setup. Sofa and bed: why a ramp beats jumping

Stairs are a few minutes a day. The sofa and the bed are where a Dachshund jumps many times a day. AKC recommends dog stairs for small breeds that have to take a running leap to settle on the sofa, because jumping up and down from those heights adds up. A ramp or a set of dog stairs gives your Dachshund the same access without the leap.

What to check before you buy

AKC lists the points that matter more than price: a slip-resistant surface, enough width so the dog does not feel it could fall off the side, a height that reaches your sofa, bed or car, a weight limit with margin, and a design that fits how you will use it (AKC: Does your dog need dog stairs or a ramp?). For a Dachshund, the surface and width are usually the deciding factors, because a short-legged dog that slips once may refuse the ramp afterwards.

How to teach your Dachshund to use a ramp

  1. Let it explore. Put the ramp flat or at a very low angle and let your Dachshund sniff it with no pressure.
  2. Lure a few steps. Use treats or a favourite toy and reward every step. Trainer Erin Askeland, quoted by AKC, recommends rewarding each step with praise and treats.
  3. Add a side barrier. Place the ramp against a wall or the sofa side so it guides the dog and it cannot leap off early. Make sure it does not wobble.
  4. Raise the angle slowly. A ramp can start low and go up as the dog gets confident — one of its advantages over stairs.
  5. Never push or pull. Forcing the dog up creates exactly the negative association you are trying to avoid.

AKC also notes that starting young is easier: a puppy that learns the ramp early never builds the habit of jumping. Once the ramp works, block the old jumping route for a while so the new one becomes the default.

FAQ

Can Dachshunds use stairs?

Many do, but breed guides advise limiting it. VCA's Dachshund breed profile says Dachshunds should not be allowed to run up and down stairs or jump off furniture, so most owners manage stairs with gates and give the dog a ramp for the sofa and bed.

Is a ramp or dog stairs better for a Dachshund?

It depends on where you use it. AKC notes that ramps fold away and travel better and suit dogs that are nervous of new things, while dog stairs take up less floor space when they stand by the sofa or bed permanently.

How do I teach my Dachshund to use a ramp?

Let the dog sniff the ramp first, start at a low angle, lure a few steps at a time with treats and praise, and never push or pull the dog onto it. A side barrier and a stable floor help.

What should I look for when buying a ramp?

A slip-resistant surface, enough width for the dog, a height that matches the sofa, bed or car, a weight limit with margin, and a design that fits where you will use it.
