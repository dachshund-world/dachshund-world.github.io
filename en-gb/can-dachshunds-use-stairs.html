Can Dachshunds Use Stairs? A UK Home Guide

Most dachshunds can physically climb stairs. The question for a UK owner is how much stairs, wooden floors and sofa leaps your dog does every day — and how to set the house up so the answer is "as little as possible".

Short answer

Let a dachshund use stairs rarely, not daily. Gate the top and bottom of the main flight, put a runner on slippery floors, and give the dog a ramp or a safe lift for the sofa and the car. It is about the total load across the week, not one climb.

Why UK homes are a particular problem

British housing stock is full of narrow, steep staircases, original floorboards and polished laminate. A dachshund's long back and short legs are a poor match for a flight of stairs with a tight turn and a hard landing, and a hallway runner is often the difference between a confident dog and one that skids into the skirting board.

Flats add another layer: shared stairs, tiled communal halls and a front door that opens straight onto a pavement. None of that is a reason to wrap the dog in cotton wool; it is a reason to plan the route.

Where to put the stair gate

Fit a gate at the bottom of the main flight first, because that is where most attempts start. A gate at the top matters if the dog sleeps upstairs or follows you up. Pressure-fit gates are the practical choice in rented homes because they need no drilling — but check the tenancy agreement before fitting anything permanent.

Make the gate boring. Feed the dog near it, leave it open at first, and never use it only when the dog is being shut away from the family.

Slips and slides: runners, rugs and carpet

Hard floors are the quiet problem. A dachshund that skids on a laminate hallway will splay its legs and tense its back several times a day. A long runner from the front door to the kitchen, a rug where the dog turns, and a mat at the bottom of the stairs do more than any gadget.

Keep the nails short and the paw fur trimmed level with the pads; a dog that cannot grip will slide even on carpet.

Carrying, ramps and teaching the route

When you lift a dachshund, support the chest and the back end together, keeping the spine level — never lift it by the front legs alone. For the sofa and the bed, a gentle ramp is usually easier than pet steps because the body stays level; for the car, a ramp or a low boot is kinder than a jump down.

Teach the ramp with treats and short sessions, starting on the floor before you raise it. A ramp the dog refuses is just furniture, so build the habit before you need it.

Rented flats and shared stairs

In a rented flat, keep the setup removable: pressure gates, washable runners, a folding ramp. Carry the dog up and down the shared flight while it is young, and choose the quieter stairwell if the building has two. If the entrance is tiled, a small mat inside the door gives the dog a landing spot to stop on.

Questions UK owners ask

Can dachshunds go up stairs but not down?
Many can climb up and hesitate on the way down. Descending loads the front of the body and the long back differently, and a steep, open staircase looks unsafe to a low dog. Gate the top, and carry or ramp the descent rather than coaxing.
Are dog stairs safer than a ramp for a dachshund?
For a long, low dog a gentle ramp is usually easier than a short flight of pet steps, because the body stays level. Pet stairs can work for one or two low steps if they are wide and carpeted; they are not a substitute for a full staircase.
Do I need a stair gate in a rented flat?
Often yes. A pressure-fit gate needs no drilling and comes down at the end of the tenancy, which matters if your agreement says no alterations. Check the tenancy terms before fitting anything permanent.
