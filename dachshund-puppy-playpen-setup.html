Dachshund Puppy Playpen Setup: Panel Height, Bar Style, Floor Space and Layout to Stop Climbing Out

A dachshund puppy playpen can turn from safe corner to climbing frame within a few weeks, usually because of one foothold nobody noticed. If you have already found your puppy on the wrong side of the wall, or you expect to, the fix is in the height you really have, the shape of the bars and where you put things.

Quick answer

Choose a rigid pen whose top edge sits well above your puppy’s reared-up paw reach, with as few horizontal footholds as possible. Put the bed at one end and the potty spot at the other, keep the crate roof and furniture from becoming a step, anchor the panels, and re-measure every week or two.

Dachshund puppy playpen planner: will it fit, and can your puppy get out?

Stand your puppy facing a wall, let it rear up for a treat, and mark where the front paws land; then enter that figure with your pen and crate sizes below.

Pen footprint and foothold checker

All sizes in inches. The starting values are examples; replace them with your own tape measurements.

The checker does two separate jobs. The footprint is plain geometry. The climb check is a rule of thumb: it subtracts whatever your puppy could stand on from the panel height, because a 30 inch panel next to an 18 inch crate is, from the puppy’s point of view, a 12 inch step.

Before you buy panels, it helps to see a pen laid out by a trainer: in this DGP Dog Behavior Videos class you will see how the confinement area is defined, what the flooring is for and how the potty spot fits in.

How tall should a dachshund puppy playpen be?

Tall enough that your puppy’s reared-up front paws stay clearly below the top edge, measured from the highest thing it can stand on. A breed label cannot give you that number, because puppies differ and grow.

What you can buy

Wire exercise pens are sold in height steps. One common design is the MidWest 8 panel exercise pen, listed with 24 inch wide panels in heights of 24, 30, 36, 42 and 48 inches, which gives 16 linear feet of wall. The same retail page shows a mesh top sold as an add-on for pets that jump or climb. Check the exact figures for the model in front of you, since brands differ.

Why the printed height is not the real height

Three things take inches away. A thick bed or folded blanket against the wall raises the floor. A crate, toy bin or litter box with raised edges gives a step. A panel that leans outward or sags between joints is lower where it bends. Subtract the tallest of these before you judge the wall.

Buying for the next few months

Your puppy will be longer, stronger and more practised later than on the day the box arrives. If you are choosing between two heights and the taller one still lets you step over or use the door comfortably, the taller one saves a second purchase. Also plan how you will get in and out: a pen you have to lean over all day invites lifting the puppy over the top, and that has its own problem, described below.

Which bar style gives a climber the fewest footholds?

Smooth panels and mostly vertical bars give less to stand on than horizontal rails or a wide square grid. We reason this from the shape of the wall; we have not tested pens, and we know of no rating system for climb resistance.

Gaps matter as much as height

A dachshund puppy is narrow. Hold a panel next to your puppy and look at whether the head or a leg could pass through or get wedged, including at the hinges and where the last panel meets the first. The door is a weak point too: check that both latches close and that the bottom lip is not so high that a short-legged puppy trips going through it.

Soft pens are not for unsupervised hours

Trainer Cathy Madson, writing for Preventive Vet, likes pop-up pens with a removable mesh cover for moving around the home with a bouncy puppy, but advises against leaving a puppy completely unsupervised in one, because a determined puppy could chew through the mesh.

How much floor space does a puppy pen need?

Room for a bed or crate, water, a separate potty spot and a bit of play, but not so much that your puppy starts toileting wherever it likes. Preventive Vet puts it the same way: size depends on the puppy, and the pen should not be so big that accidents happen outside the potty area.

Start smaller, then widen

If your puppy keeps its bed dry and uses the tray, you can add space gradually as it matures. If it starts using a free corner as a second toilet, take the space back or move the tray. Keep the crate itself small: our dachshund crate size calculator works out the inside length and where a divider should go.

Where do the crate, water and potty tray go?

Rest area at one end, potty spot as far from it as the pen allows, water near the bed, chews in the middle. That arrangement matches the advice from trainers and shelters alike.

Rest end

Leave the crate door open so the puppy can choose to sleep there. A long-term confinement handout from SPCA of Wake County lists the open crate with soft bedding, water and food bowls, and chew toys safe to leave unsupervised. If you skip the crate, give a comfortable bed in one corner. A heavy bed or one you can secure is harder to drag to the wall and use as a boost.

Potty end

Use a tray, pads or a turf square, whichever matches your house-training plan. A tray with raised edges helps stop toys rolling into the toilet area, but count its rim as a possible step and keep it a little away from the panel if your puppy likes to perch on it. If you are aiming for outdoor toileting only, think carefully before covering the whole floor in pads.

Middle

This is where the puppy chews and plays. Stuffed food toys give it something to do besides studying the fence. Our dachshund toys guide has more ideas, and a puppy that loves to burrow may later enjoy a supervised digging box outside the pen, where it cannot become a platform.

Which room

Pick an easy-to-clean floor, clear of cords, furniture and anything you do not want chewed. Kitchens, laundry rooms and bathrooms are the usual choices because a door or baby gate can back up the pen. Make sure no sofa, chair, shelf or windowsill is within reach from the top of a panel or from the crate.

How do you stop a dachshund puppy climbing out of the playpen?

Remove the step first, then the footholds, then add height, and stop rewarding the exit. Watch one escape if you safely can: the route tells you which of these to fix.

1. Move the step

The crate roof is the classic launch pad. Instead of standing the crate in the middle or against an inside wall, attach it so only the door opening faces into the pen and the body sits outside, using the clips the maker supplies. Push toy bins and tall water stations out of the setup.

2. Cover or change the rungs

If the puppy is using horizontal wires, switch to a panel style with fewer of them, or fix a smooth, chew-resistant sheet flat against the inside of the section it climbs. Check it every day: nothing should be loose, sharp or chewable, and there must still be airflow.

3. Make the top hard to grip

One trainer’s account of a puppy that climbed out on its first night suggests cutting a thin PVC pipe lengthwise and fitting it along the top of the fence, so there is a rounded edge that is harder to hook paws over. The same writer warns that a dog going over a fence can catch its paws on the way.

4. Use the door, not the airlift

In that account, the owner realised they had been lifting the puppy over the wall instead of using the door, and so had been showing it that the way out was over the top. Open the door, ask for a moment of calm, then let your puppy walk out. Reward four paws on the floor while it is inside.

The same article describes tapping the fence just as the puppy rears up so that it gets down. We would not start there: startling a young dog can make some puppies worried about the pen. Rewarding the calm moments is a gentler first step.

5. Anchor the walls

A light pen can be pushed across a smooth floor, opened into a gap or tipped. Preventive Vet advises securing an exercise pen to the crate or setting it against a wall and other anchor points so the puppy cannot knock it over and get out.

6. When it is still not enough

If your puppy clears a sensible height with no step to help, stop relying on the pen while you are away. Use a closed room with a solid gate it cannot jump or climb, or another supervised arrangement, until you have a taller or covered setup. A dachshund that learns to get out may next head for the sofa or the stairs; see can dachshunds use stairs for how to manage those.

Playpen setup checklist, step by step

  1. Measure the puppy. Note nose-to-tail-base length and reared-up paw reach. Write the date next to both.
  2. Measure the room. Tape the pen outline on the floor and check doors still open and you can walk around it.
  3. Choose the wall. Pick height and bar style from the checks above, and confirm the panel width so the footprint is what you expect.
  4. Clear a halo. Move furniture, cords, bins and plants out of reach from all sides.
  5. Protect the floor. Use a washable, non-slip surface the puppy cannot easily pull up and chew.
  6. Anchor. Join the first and last panel, use two walls or the crate for support, and secure both door latches.
  7. Place the zones. Bed at one end, potty spot at the other, water by the bed, chews in between.
  8. Push test. Lean on each panel at puppy height. If it slides or folds, fix it now.
  9. Supervised first sessions. Stay nearby and watch where your puppy tries its paws.
  10. Re-check weekly. Run the planner again with the new reach figure and look for chewed clips or loose ties.

Helping your puppy settle so it has less reason to leave

Even a good layout fails if the pen only ever means being left alone. Introduce it in small pieces. Take your puppy out for a toilet break, then place it in the pen with a stuffed food toy. Leave it for a few minutes while you stay at home, and let it out calmly, ideally before the toy is finished. Repeat several times a day and lengthen the time gradually.

The SPCA handout suggests starting after about five minutes and then varying absences from 30 seconds to 20 minutes. If barking or crying starts, it advises waiting for at least 10 seconds of quiet before you go in, so noise does not become the way to get the door opened. Preventive Vet adds that calm reassurance is fine and that you should not simply leave a distressed puppy to cry it out, so read your own dog and go back to a shorter step if it is panicking.

A little play before pen time helps many puppies nap. For nights and crate protests, see our guides to a puppy that will not sleep in the crate and bedtime crate and separation anxiety.

When to use the pen and when the crate

The pen is for periods longer than your puppy can manage in a closed crate, and for moments such as mealtimes, visitors or a work call. The crate is for short rests. Neither replaces toilet trips, training and time with you.

Common playpen mistakes

  • Judging by the box height. A bed or crate beside the wall removes inches.
  • Crate in the middle. It splits the floor and offers a platform.
  • Too much space too soon. A spare corner often becomes a toilet.
  • Bed next to the potty spot. Puppies generally try to keep their sleeping area clean, so give them the distance to do it.
  • Free-standing panels on tile. A puppy can shove them until a gap opens.
  • Lifting over the top. It rehearses the exit you are trying to prevent.
  • Leaving collars, tags or loose ties where they can snag. Check what your puppy wears and what you have added to the wall before leaving it.
  • Using the pen as punishment. A puppy that dreads it will work harder to get out.
  • Never re-measuring. The pen that held last month was measured against a smaller dog.

Dachshund puppy playpen FAQ

How tall should a dachshund puppy playpen be?
There is no single correct number. Measure how high your puppy’s front paws reach when reared up, subtract the height of anything it can climb on near the wall, and keep the top edge clearly above that reach. Many owners start at 24 or 30 inches and go taller if the puppy tests the wall.
How much floor space does a dachshund puppy need in a pen?
Enough for a crate or bed, water, a potty spot well away from the bed, and a small play strip. Eight panels that are each 24 inches wide make a square of 4 by 4 feet, or 16 square feet, which you can then shrink or enlarge by watching where your puppy toilets.
Should the crate go inside the playpen?
It can. An open crate inside the pen gives the puppy a rest area it can choose. Keep it where its roof cannot be used as a step over the wall, for example attached outside the pen with only the door facing in.
Are vertical bars better than horizontal bars?
For a climber, plain vertical bars or a smooth panel give fewer places to put a paw than horizontal rails or a wide square grid. This is a matter of shape rather than a tested rating, so watch your own puppy.
Can I put a lid on a puppy pen?
Some pop-up pens come with a removable mesh cover. Do not leave a puppy completely unsupervised in a soft portable pen, because a determined one can chew through the mesh.
How long can a dachshund puppy stay in a playpen?
The pen is for stretches longer than your puppy can stay in a closed crate, with water and a toilet spot available. It is not a substitute for breaks, play and company, and you should shorten the stretch if your puppy is distressed.

Summary: a dachshund puppy playpen that keeps working

Judge your dachshund puppy playpen by the barrier the puppy actually faces, not the number on the box. Pick a wall with few footholds, give the bed and potty spot opposite ends, keep anything climbable away from the edge, anchor the panels and use the door. Then measure again in a week or two. If one escape route appears, close that one first and watch the next session before you leave the room.
