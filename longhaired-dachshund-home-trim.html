How to Trim a Longhaired Dachshund at Home: Feathers, Paws, Ears and Tail

Preserve the flowing coat and build a lighter routine: one visible edge, one manageable task, then a break.

Light outline maintenanceInteractive tidy boardOctober 15, 2026

A manageable way to learn how to trim a longhaired dachshund at home is to separate everyday cleanup from an occasional light tidy. You can keep the paws neat and the feathering practical without turning every muddy walk into a full haircut or cutting away the coat you love.

Quick answer

Start with a clean, dry, comfortably combed coat. Tidy only clearly visible hair extending beyond the natural foot outline or the existing feathering edge, taking small snips and combing again between them. Preserve the ear fringe and tail flag; leave tight tangles, hidden pad hair, and close work beneath the ears to a groomer. Finish one manageable area, then take a break.

Build your post-walk tidy board

Choose the areas that need attention and what you found there. This board separates cleanup from trimming; it cannot judge your dog's coat or confirm that a cutting technique is appropriate.

Areas you want to check





What should you trim, and what should you leave?

A home maintenance trim tidies protruding ends while preserving the natural outline. It does not require shortening the back, removing all feathering, or making every part of the coat the same length.

The Royal Kennel Club's longhaired dachshund standard, updated in April 2025, describes feathering beneath the body and behind the legs, feathered outer ears, and a tail flag. It also calls excessive foot hair undesirable. That is an appearance reference—not an instruction to reproduce a show groom at home.

Set a practical target

Describe the problem before picking up scissors: “These foot fringes trail outside the paw,” or “These few feather tips repeatedly drag through the same patch of mud.” If you cannot identify a specific problem, comb the area and leave its length alone.

How do you prepare without making grooming exhausting?

Prepare one small task, the tools for that task, and a clear stopping point. Comfortable cooperation and a freely combed section come before any cutting.

Build a simple working space

Use good light and a stable, nonslip surface where you can see the selected area. A floor-level arrangement is practical if you can work without stretching the dog or leaning over them. Put down tools before repositioning your dachshund.

Have a brush suitable for the coat, a metal comb, a towel, and the grooming scissors your demonstrator showed you. Rounded tips do not make uncertain cutting harmless. Keep the scissors closed and aside while brushing, giving a reward, or reaching for another tool.

Practice the handling separately

Start with touching the area, a brief brush stroke, and a pause. If your dog turns away or repeatedly withdraws a foot, put the cutting plan aside. Choose a quieter setting if activity outside keeps interrupting; our dachshund barking guide explains how to organize practice around environmental distractions.

In Fear Free's brushing demonstration, Mikkel Becker and Debbie Martin show short, rewarding handling sessions and allowing the animal breaks. This September 2021 video helps with the preparation stage; it demonstrates comfortable brushing, not dachshund scissoring.

A reward should accompany comfortable handling, rather than distract your dog while you carry on with something they are trying to avoid. End the session when cooperation ends, even if you planned to complete another area.

How do you tidy longhaired dachshund paws step by step?

Separate the outer foot outline from the underside. A light outline tidy is a smaller task than clearing hair between pads, and learning one does not mean you have learned the other.

In her July 2026 paw-trimming guide, groomer Lucy distinguishes pad work from foot shaping. Her outline method uses combing, small snips, and the natural foot shape; she also emphasizes comfortable positioning and not digging into the spaces between toes.

Work around the visible outline

  1. Clean and dry the foot. Do not choose a cutting line through stuck mud. Separate removable debris gently before checking the hair.
  2. Comb through comfortably. Bring the long outer hairs into view. If the comb stops at a tight knot, leave that patch for a groomer.
  3. Let the foot rest naturally. With the dog standing comfortably, look at the outline against the surface. Identify only hair projecting beyond that outline.
  4. Take small perimeter snips. Follow the curve of the foot using the technique you were shown. Keep the tips directed away from the toes; stop before repositioning the dog.
  5. Comb again and check. Return the hair to its natural position. Remove an obvious remaining stray only if the dog stays settled and you can see exactly what you are cutting.
  6. Pause before the next foot. Do not keep shortening one side while trying to make all feet perfectly identical.

Keep underside work separate

Do not insert scissors between the pads or probe beneath a tangle. Ask a groomer to demonstrate any underside maintenance on your dog, including whether a clipper tidy is appropriate. Until then, the foot outline can be your completed home task.

How much leg and belly feathering should you remove?

For a first tidy, remove only isolated free ends that you can clearly separate from the body and identify as the problem. Keep the longer feathering and review the outline with the dog standing normally.

Leg feathers: preserve the fringe

Comb the fringe behind the leg gently downward into its normal fall. Look for a few tips extending beyond the established outline rather than treating every longer strand as excess. Have the groomer show you how to hold the free hair away from the leg and keep your fingers between the intended cut and the body.

Trim only those demonstrated, isolated tips in small snips. Release the hair, comb again, and reassess. Avoid turning a natural fringe into a straight horizontal shelf. If you want substantial shortening, ask for a professional outline trim and learn the maintenance after it.

Belly fringe: inspect from the side

With your dog standing, comb the hanging ends into place and view the underline from the side. Do not lift the entire skirt and cut across it in one pass. Only clearly separated free tips belong in a beginner's light tidy; scissors should not travel against the belly, into the armpits, or through folds.

Remove walking equipment before checking the hair it covers. Our dachshund harness guide addresses walking gear; for this task, having the straps out of the way lets you examine the coat underneath.

Can you trim the ear fringe without changing the look?

You can keep the ears tidy by combing and preserving the fringe. Cutting around an ear requires a clear understanding of where the ear itself ends, so a first attempt should follow a demonstration on your dog.

Comb first and identify the boundary

Support the ear gently while combing the external hair in its natural direction. Work patiently through loose ends, without dragging a knot down the fringe. Feel the edge of the ear with your fingers; long hair can conceal it.

If a groomer has shown you a small fringe tidy, copy only the clearly separated hair ends they identified beyond the ear itself. Do not use the apparent hair outline as proof that tissue is out of the way. If you cannot keep that boundary clear throughout the movement, finish at combing.

Leave hidden areas alone

This project covers external coat maintenance. Do not put scissors inside the ear, pull hair from the canal, or improvise close trimming behind the ear. A groomer can assess a tight tangle there without making it part of your home scissoring lesson.

The aim is a feathered ear that falls naturally. “Neater” does not require shortening it to the ear's edge or matching a completely different breed's ear style.

How do you keep the tail flag neat?

Comb the flag before deciding that it needs cutting. Preserve its length and natural fall, tidying only clearly identified stray ends rather than creating a new tail shape.

The Dachshund Club of America's 2013 handbook separates pet longhair maintenance from show grooming. Its coat description emphasizes the tail's long flag. Use that distinction to set a modest pet-maintenance target rather than following advanced show-finishing instructions.

Check the fall before making a cut

Support the tail without lifting it into an unnatural position. Comb the hanging hair, then let the tail return to its usual carriage and look again. A strand that appears excessive while you hold the tail up may sit differently when the dog moves.

After a hands-on demonstration, isolate only an obvious free tip that disrupts the existing flag and trim minimally. Comb and release again. Do not twist the hair into a rope, cut the whole flag straight across, or cut close to the tail itself to remove a hidden knot.

Save a major restyle for another appointment

If you want a shorter flag, describe how much of the flowing look you want to keep and ask the groomer to establish the outline. Your maintenance task afterward is easier to define: preserve that shape and keep it combed.

A lighter routine for muddy walks and busy weeks

Make cleanup the regular habit and trimming the occasional decision. The routine below is an editorial planning template, not a breed-specific timetable or a promised grooming duration.

  1. At the door: check paws, hanging feathering, outer ears, and the tail for visible debris. Remove loose material only when it separates comfortably.
  2. Before combing: clean muddy areas and let the coat dry appropriately. Do not use scissors as a shortcut through dirt or a snag.
  3. During the check: work on a small section and stop if you cannot comb it comfortably. Record a tight tangle for the groomer rather than working around it blindly.
  4. At a separate tidy session: choose one specific outline problem and decide whether it can be solved by combing alone.
  5. At the end: put the tools away, note what you finished, and leave the remaining tasks for another comfortable session.

Keep a short record such as “front foot outlines finished; ears combed; tail left at full length.” This is a suggested note format, not a reported grooming result. It helps another household member avoid trimming the same area again.

Common mistakes that create more work

A sustainable routine needs a stopping rule. Once the selected problem is addressed, extra cutting usually means you have started a different project.

  • Cutting every time you find mud. Clean and comb before deciding whether length is the actual problem.
  • Chasing symmetry. Compare the overall outline instead of shortening one side repeatedly to match the other.
  • Using scissors on a tight mat. Leave close tangles to a groomer rather than sliding blades underneath them.
  • Removing all the feathering. Define a small maintenance goal before making the first cut.
  • Copying wirehair grooming. This longhair routine is different from our wirehaired dachshund hand-stripping guide; do not transfer outer-coat plucking to this project.
  • Finishing through resistance. Put the scissors down and return to handling practice or professional help.

Longhaired dachshund trimming FAQ

Do I have to shorten the whole body coat?
No. This maintenance routine focuses on the foot outline and selected free ends. Leave the body length and natural feathering unless you have agreed on a different grooming plan.
Can I cut out a tight knot behind an ear?
Leave it for a groomer. Do not slide scissors beneath a tight tangle or assume that the hair hides no skin.
Should I trim between the paw pads with scissors?
Not as part of this beginner outline tidy. Keep scissors out of the gaps and ask a groomer to demonstrate any underside maintenance separately.
How short should the belly fringe be?
There is no universal length in this guide. Begin by combing it into its natural fall and identifying the specific trailing ends you want tidied; get help for substantial shortening.
Does muddy feathering always need trimming?
No. Clean, dry, and comb first. Consider a small tidy only if the same identifiable ends remain a practical problem.
How often should I do a complete home trim?
Do not make a full trim the default. Check the coat regularly, choose small maintenance tasks when needed, and agree on a grooming interval for your individual dog.

Keep the coat, simplify the routine

Learning how to trim a longhaired dachshund at home works best as a series of small, well-defined tasks. Keep the foot outline practical, preserve the ear fringe and tail flag, and let combing do the work before considering scissors. A comfortable pause is part of finishing well.
